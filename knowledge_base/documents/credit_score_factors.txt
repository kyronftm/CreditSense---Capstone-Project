CREDIT SCORE FACTORS AND METHODOLOGY — COMPREHENSIVE GUIDE
Based on FICO Score Model and Official CFPB/Federal Reserve Guidance

CREDIT SCORE RANGES (FICO Score 8):
- Exceptional/Excellent: 800-850
- Very Good: 740-799
- Good: 670-739
- Fair: 580-669
- Poor: 300-579

THE FIVE FACTORS THAT DETERMINE YOUR CREDIT SCORE:

1. PAYMENT HISTORY (35% of FICO Score)
This is the single most important factor in credit scoring.
- On-time payments: Consistently paying all bills on or before the due date is the best way to maintain a strong payment history.
- Late payments: A single late payment can lower a credit score significantly, especially for consumers with otherwise clean histories. The impact depends on:
  - How late the payment was (30, 60, 90, 120+ days)
  - How recently the late payment occurred
  - How many late payments exist
- Collections: Accounts sent to collections remain on credit reports for 7 years from the date of first delinquency.
- Public records: Bankruptcies (Chapter 7 remains 10 years, Chapter 13 remains 7 years), foreclosures (7 years), tax liens.
- Recovery: The negative impact of late payments diminishes over time. After 12-24 months of on-time payments, the impact decreases substantially.

2. AMOUNTS OWED / CREDIT UTILIZATION (30% of FICO Score)
Credit utilization ratio is the percentage of available credit being used.
- Recommended utilization: Below 30% is generally recommended; below 10% is optimal for the highest scores.
- Per-card and overall: Both individual card utilization and total utilization across all revolving accounts matter.
- High utilization impact: Using more than 50% of available credit significantly reduces scores.
- Zero utilization: Having 0% utilization (no balances) may not produce the highest scores. A small utilization (1-5%) may be optimal.
- Installment loan balances: The ratio of remaining balance to original loan amount also matters for installment loans.
- Reducing utilization: This is one of the fastest ways to improve a credit score. Reducing balances can show improvement within 1-2 billing cycles.

3. LENGTH OF CREDIT HISTORY (15% of FICO Score)
Longer credit history generally results in higher scores.
- Average age of accounts: Calculated across all open accounts.
- Age of oldest account: Having a long-standing account demonstrates credit management experience.
- Age of newest account: Very new accounts lower the average age.
- Why NOT to close old accounts: Closing old accounts reduces average account age and total credit limit, potentially hurting the score.
- Building history: It takes at least 6 months of credit history to generate a FICO score.

4. CREDIT MIX (10% of FICO Score)
Having a diverse mix of credit types can help the score.
- Types of credit:
  - Revolving credit (credit cards, home equity lines)
  - Installment loans (mortgages, auto loans, student loans, personal loans)
  - Open accounts (charge cards paid in full each month)
- Optimal mix: Having both revolving and installment credit demonstrates the ability to manage different types of debt.
- Not essential: A consumer does not need one of each type. Do NOT open new accounts solely for mix purposes.

5. NEW CREDIT / INQUIRIES (10% of FICO Score)
Opening many new accounts in a short period increases risk.
- Hard inquiries: Occur when a lender checks credit for a lending decision. Each hard inquiry can temporarily lower the score by 5-10 points.
- Soft inquiries: Do NOT affect the score (employer checks, personal credit checks, pre-approved offers).
- Rate shopping: Multiple inquiries for the same type of loan (mortgage, auto, student) within a 14-45 day window count as a single inquiry.
- Duration: Hard inquiries affect the score for 12 months but remain visible on the report for 2 years.
- New accounts: Opening several accounts in a short period looks risky, especially for consumers with short credit histories.

CREDIT SCORE IMPROVEMENT STRATEGIES BY TIER:

FOR POOR CREDIT (300-579):
- Focus on making all payments on time going forward
- Address any accounts in collections — consider negotiating pay-for-delete agreements
- Consider a secured credit card to build positive payment history
- Become an authorized user on a family member's well-managed credit card
- Check credit reports for errors and dispute any inaccuracies
- Expected improvement timeline: 6-18 months for significant improvement

FOR FAIR CREDIT (580-669):
- Prioritize reducing credit card balances below 30% utilization
- Set up automatic payments to avoid future late payments
- Do not apply for new credit unless absolutely necessary
- Keep all existing accounts open, even if not used regularly
- Dispute any errors on credit reports
- Expected improvement timeline: 3-12 months for 20-50 point improvement

FOR GOOD CREDIT (670-739):
- Optimize utilization below 10% on all cards
- Continue consistent on-time payment track record
- Avoid opening unnecessary new accounts
- Let credit history age naturally
- Monitor credit reports regularly for accuracy
- Expected improvement timeline: 6-12 months for 20-40 point improvement

FOR VERY GOOD CREDIT (740-799):
- Maintain low utilization (under 10%)
- Keep oldest accounts active
- Limit hard inquiries to necessary applications
- Maintain a healthy credit mix
- Continue monitoring for any errors
- Expected improvement timeline: 12+ months for incremental gains

SCORE RECOVERY TIMELINES:
- Late payment (30 days): 1-2 years to fully recover
- Late payment (90 days): 3-7 years to fully recover
- Collection account: 3-7 years (impact diminishes over time)
- Bankruptcy (Chapter 7): 7-10 years
- Bankruptcy (Chapter 13): 5-7 years
- Hard inquiry: 12 months impact, 2 years on report
- High utilization: 1-2 billing cycles after paying down
