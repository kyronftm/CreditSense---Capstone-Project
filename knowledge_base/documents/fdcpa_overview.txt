FAIR DEBT COLLECTION PRACTICES ACT (FDCPA) — OVERVIEW
15 U.S.C. § 1692 et seq.

The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) restricts the practices of third-party debt collectors when collecting consumer debts. It provides consumers with rights to dispute debts and limits abusive collection practices.

COVERED DEBTS:
The FDCPA covers personal, family, and household debts including:
- Credit card debt
- Auto loans
- Medical debt
- Mortgages
- Student loans (private)

WHO IS COVERED:
The FDCPA applies to third-party debt collectors — companies or individuals who regularly collect debts owed to others. Original creditors collecting their own debts are generally NOT covered (but may be covered by state laws).

CONSUMER RIGHTS UNDER FDCPA:

1. RIGHT TO VALIDATION OF DEBT
Within 5 days of first contact, a debt collector must send a written validation notice including:
- The amount of the debt
- The name of the creditor
- A statement that the consumer has 30 days to dispute the debt
- A statement that if disputed, the collector will provide verification

2. RIGHT TO DISPUTE
Consumers can dispute a debt within 30 days of receiving the validation notice. Upon receiving a written dispute, the collector must:
- Cease collection activity on the disputed amount
- Obtain verification of the debt
- Mail verification to the consumer

3. RIGHT TO REQUEST CEASE COMMUNICATION
Consumers can request in writing that a debt collector stop all communication. After receiving such a request, the collector can only contact the consumer to:
- Advise that collection efforts are being terminated
- Notify of a specific intended action (e.g., lawsuit)

PROHIBITED PRACTICES:

Harassment or Abuse:
- Threats of violence or harm
- Use of obscene or profane language
- Repeated phone calls intended to annoy
- Publishing lists of consumers who allegedly refuse to pay debts

False or Misleading Representations:
- Falsely implying they are attorneys or government representatives
- Misrepresenting the amount owed
- Threatening actions they cannot legally take
- Falsely implying the consumer committed a crime

Unfair Practices:
- Collecting amounts not authorized by the agreement or law
- Depositing post-dated checks prematurely
- Communicating with consumers at inconvenient times (before 8 AM or after 9 PM)
- Contacting consumers at their workplace if the employer disapproves

IMPACT ON CREDIT REPORTS:
Debt collectors who report to credit bureaus must:
- Report accurate information
- Notify the bureau if the consumer disputes the debt
- Report the debt as disputed if the consumer has filed a dispute
- Remove inaccurate information from credit reports

STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS:
The FDCPA has a one-year statute of limitations for consumers to file lawsuits. However, state laws may provide longer periods. The statute of limitations on the underlying debt varies by state (typically 3-6 years).

REMEDIES:
Consumers who sue and win can recover:
- Actual damages (emotional distress, lost wages, etc.)
- Statutory damages up to $1,000 per lawsuit
- Attorney's fees and court costs
