EQUAL CREDIT OPPORTUNITY ACT (ECOA) — OVERVIEW
15 U.S.C. § 1691 et seq. | Regulation B (12 CFR Part 1002)

The Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) prohibits discrimination in any aspect of a credit transaction. It applies to any extension of credit, including personal, commercial, and mortgage credit.

PROHIBITED DISCRIMINATION BASES:
Creditors may NOT discriminate against credit applicants on the basis of:
- Race or color
- Religion
- National origin
- Sex (including gender identity and sexual orientation)
- Marital status
- Age (provided the applicant has the capacity to contract)
- Receipt of income from any public assistance program
- Good faith exercise of any right under the Consumer Credit Protection Act

CREDITOR OBLIGATIONS UNDER ECOA:
1. NOTIFICATION OF ACTION TAKEN
Creditors must notify applicants of action taken on their applications within 30 days of receiving a completed application. If credit is denied or other adverse action is taken, the notice must include:
- A statement of the action taken
- The specific reasons for the action (or a notice of the applicant's right to request such reasons)
- The ECOA anti-discrimination notice

2. PROHIBITED INQUIRIES
Creditors generally cannot ask about:
- Plans to have children or use birth control
- Marital status (in certain situations)
- Race, color, religion, or national origin (except for monitoring purposes)

3. INCOME CONSIDERATIONS
Creditors must consider all income stated by an applicant, including part-time employment, social security, pensions, and alimony/child support payments.

4. SPOUSAL REQUIREMENTS
A creditor generally may not require a spouse to co-sign for individual credit, if the applicant qualifies on their own.

IMPACT ON CREDIT SCORING:
Credit scoring systems used by creditors must not use prohibited factors. Systems must be empirically derived and statistically sound. Age may be used as a factor only if it does not disadvantage applicants aged 62 or older.

ENFORCEMENT:
ECOA is enforced by the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), Federal Trade Commission (FTC), and other federal agencies. Consumers may also file private lawsuits.
