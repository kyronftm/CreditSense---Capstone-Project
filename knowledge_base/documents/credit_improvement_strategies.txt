CREDIT IMPROVEMENT STRATEGIES — OFFICIAL GUIDANCE
Based on CFPB, Federal Reserve, and FTC Consumer Education Materials

OVERVIEW:
Improving a credit score is a process that takes time and consistent effort. There are no legitimate quick fixes. Any company promising to rapidly improve a credit score is likely engaging in deceptive practices.

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS (1-2 Billing Cycles):

1. PAY DOWN REVOLVING BALANCES
- Impact: Can improve score by 20-60 points depending on current utilization
- Target: Reduce each card below 30% utilization; ideally below 10%
- Strategy: Pay more than the minimum on highest-utilization cards first
- Note: Paying before the statement closing date ensures lower utilization is reported

2. REQUEST CREDIT LIMIT INCREASES
- Impact: Reduces utilization ratio without paying down balances
- Caution: This may trigger a hard inquiry depending on the issuer
- Best for: Consumers with good payment history who haven't requested increases recently

3. BECOME AN AUTHORIZED USER
- Impact: Can add years of positive history and increase available credit
- Requirements: The primary cardholder should have excellent payment history and low utilization
- Note: Not all card issuers report authorized user accounts to all bureaus

SHORT-TERM ACTIONS (3-6 Months):

4. SET UP AUTOMATIC PAYMENTS
- Impact: Prevents future late payments, which have the highest negative impact
- Strategy: Set up autopay for at least the minimum payment on all accounts
- Note: Even one 30-day late payment can drop a score 60-100 points

5. DISPUTE CREDIT REPORT ERRORS
- Process: File disputes with each bureau that shows inaccurate information
- Timeline: Bureaus must investigate within 30 days (45 days in some cases)
- Common errors: Wrong payment status, incorrect balances, accounts that don't belong to you, incorrect personal information
- Impact: Removing negative errors can improve score significantly

6. NEGOTIATE WITH CREDITORS
- Pay-for-delete: Request that a creditor remove a collection account from your report upon full payment
- Goodwill adjustments: Request removal of a single late payment if you have otherwise good history
- Settlement: Negotiate to pay less than the full balance (note: settled accounts may still show as negative)

MEDIUM-TERM ACTIONS (6-12 Months):

7. BUILD POSITIVE PAYMENT HISTORY
- Consistency: Six months of on-time payments begins to show meaningful improvement
- Strategy: Use credit cards for small recurring charges and pay in full each month
- Impact: Each month of on-time payments adds to the positive record

8. DIVERSIFY CREDIT MIX
- If you only have credit cards: Consider a credit-builder loan from a credit union
- If you only have installment loans: Consider a secured credit card
- Caution: Do NOT open new accounts solely for mix — only if you genuinely need the credit

9. REDUCE HARD INQUIRIES
- Strategy: Avoid applying for new credit for 6-12 months
- Rate shopping: If applying for a mortgage or auto loan, do all applications within a 14-day window
- Impact: Each hard inquiry reduces score by 5-10 points

LONG-TERM STRATEGIES (12+ Months):

10. KEEP OLD ACCOUNTS OPEN
- Why: Average account age and oldest account age both factor into the score
- Strategy: Use old cards for a small purchase every 3-6 months to keep them active
- Risk: Closing old accounts reduces total available credit (hurts utilization) and average account age

11. MONITOR CREDIT REGULARLY
- Free tools: AnnualCreditReport.com provides free reports from all three bureaus
- Monitoring services: Consider free credit monitoring through your bank or credit card
- Frequency: Review reports at least once every 4 months (rotating between bureaus)

12. ADDRESS COLLECTIONS STRATEGICALLY
- Statute of limitations: After 7 years, collections should fall off your credit report
- Recent vs. old collections: Paying recent collections has more impact than paying very old ones
- Medical debt: Under current rules, medical debts under $500 and paid medical debts should not appear on credit reports

PRACTICES TO AVOID:

1. DO NOT close old credit card accounts (reduces average age and total credit limit)
2. DO NOT apply for multiple credit cards in a short period
3. DO NOT max out credit cards, even if you pay the full balance (utilization is reported before payment)
4. DO NOT pay for credit repair services that promise quick fixes
5. DO NOT ignore small debts — even small collections can significantly impact scores
6. DO NOT co-sign loans unless absolutely necessary (you are equally responsible)
7. DO NOT rely solely on minimum payments (carries high interest and keeps utilization high)

CONSUMER PROTECTION RESOURCES:
- CFPB: consumerfinance.gov (file complaints, get information)
- FTC: ftc.gov/credit (credit-related resources)
- AnnualCreditReport.com (free credit reports)
- Consumer Action: consumer-action.org (multilingual resources)
- National Foundation for Credit Counseling: nfcc.org (nonprofit credit counseling)

WARNING ABOUT CREDIT REPAIR COMPANIES:
Under the Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA), credit repair companies:
- Cannot require upfront payment before performing services
- Must provide a written contract with cancellation rights
- Cannot make false claims about their services
- Must inform consumers of their right to dispute information themselves for free
- Consumers have a 3-day right to cancel any credit repair contract
